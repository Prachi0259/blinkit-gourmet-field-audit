A field note on Blinkit Gourmet
DLF Phase 1 · Gurugram
Assortment field check

I searched Blinkit for three gourmet brands. None of them turned up.

Same zone, same afternoon, three searches. Each one came back with "Oops! We couldn't find products matching" and a request button. The screenshots are below, exactly as the app showed them.

Prachi · Checked live on the Blinkit app · Delivery zone set to DLF Phase 1

This started as ordinary curiosity. DLF Phase 1 is the kind of micro-market where people already buy specialty coffee and craft chocolate offline, so I wanted to see how deep the Gourmet assortment actually goes here. I picked brands with real followings and typed them into search, one by one.

Three of them returned nothing at all. Not out of stock, not hidden in a subcategory. Zero results. That distinction matters, because an out-of-stock badge is a replenishment problem, while a zero-result search means the brand was never onboarded to this zone in the first place. One is fixed by the supply chain. The other is fixed by a category team.

1

Cohoma

Indian specialty coffee · Ghaziabad based

A homegrown specialty roaster that closed a ₹5 crore seed round in April 2026 and has built its whole product line around brewing without equipment. Their buyer is precisely the person ordering on Blinkit at 8 am before a work call. As far as I could find, no quick commerce platform carries them yet, which makes this a first-mover shelf.

Tap to enlarge. Search term: cohoma
What the app said "We couldn't find products matching cohoma." The request-a-product screen, not an empty shelf. The brand is not in this zone's catalog.
2

Percol

Fairtrade certified UK coffee

A recognised ethical-premium coffee label. The shopper who cares about Fairtrade certification skews urban, 25 to 40, and pays for positioning rather than price. That is a strong match for this pin code, and the brand's absence from search means that shopper currently has nowhere to land.

Tap to enlarge. Search term: percol
What the app said Same zero-result screen. Nothing to add to cart, nothing to restock. A listing gap, not a stock gap.
3

Cacao Springs

Craft single-origin chocolate

Craft chocolate sits in a sweet spot for quick commerce: high gifting value, strong repeat indulgence behaviour, and none of the cold-chain complexity that keeps harder categories off the shelf. A late evening chocolate craving is exactly the impulse a ten-minute promise is built to catch.

Tap to enlarge. Search term: cacao springs
What the app said Zero results again. Three searches, three request-a-product screens, one afternoon.
Why I did this

Onboarding gaps are invisible in dashboards. You find them by searching like a customer.

A missing brand never shows up in a stockout report, because there is no SKU to report on. The only way to catch it is to stand in a zone, think about who lives there, and check the shelf the way a shopper would. That is the daily work of a Category Associate, and it is the work I have been doing on my own time. I would rather show it than describe it.
